Question Answer Where
Are Dominicans (and others) retiring back to the DR in growing numbers? The measurable retiree flow is flat, not growing: about 7,500 US Social Security beneficiaries live in the DR, within 7,176-7,696 every year since 2009 [S158]. New residencies for all foreigners were 7,110 in 2024, of which 212 on the investor, pensioner and rentista track; new US-citizen residencies are roughly 250-400 a year [S104, S171]. The diaspora is large (3.03 M) and ageing (median age 46 in the US) [S076, S009]. 2
What do these buyers want, and how is it changing? The channel that grows is the diaspora buying a finished home while still abroad: the Banreservas New York fair sold 824 homes in March 2026, 529 of them finished, and 64 % of the financing went to finished units [S179]. Wealthy Dominican families own Jarabacoa's villas and the town's tourism works about 12 days a month [S046, S126]. Buyers want single-level, low-maintenance, secure, serviced homes with a community and a clinic nearby; returnees are pulled by family, not by retirement itself [S003, S061]. 3
Lot only, lot plus builder options, or turnkey? The Dominican mountain norm is a lot plus developer-offered villa models with staged payments [S084, S085, S043]. Recommendation: two offers, a lot-plus-house package with a fixed-price contract for the diaspora, and design-coded lots with dues-based holding for capital families; developer-built model villas only against contracts; no penalty clock. 4
What do Costa Rica and the pioneers teach? Scale takes decades (Atenas has about 2,500 expats; Chapala's community is a century old), water rights and title gate every sale, resale takes a year, dues run US$100-500 a month, and crime and price displacement now push people out (873 homicides in 2025, 16.7 per 100,000) [S114, S112, S022, S135, S189]. 5
Who are the competitors and at what price? Jarabacoa serviced lots list at a median US$65/m² and US$85-110 in central gated projects; villas US$160-350 k in volume, US$500 k-1.2 M at the top [S046, S043, S167, S109]. Constanza Hills, the only new large-lot amenitised project, sells 2,000 m² lots at US$45-55/m² with 30 % down [S034]. Rancho Arriba's one lot project asks US$70/m² unserviced with US$50-100 monthly dues [S036]. 6
What project would work at Rancho Arriba with high margins? A 42-lot gated village with model villas is profitable only if it reaches Jarabacoa prices and sells six or more lots a year. At today's local prices it loses money; at parity it earns US$0.5-0.8 M after tax and financing on a peak cash need of US$1.9-2.8 M; the neighbour's flat land adds more value per dollar than the family field. The honest headline is a thin return unless the premium is proven in phase 1 (derived, section 8). 7-8

0. Executive summary

  1. The retiree flow is real but flat. The US Social Security Administration pays 7,499 beneficiaries at Dominican addresses (5,253 retired workers), US$8.4 M a month; the count has stayed between 7,176 and 7,696 since 2009 while the money rose 64 % [S158, S159]. About 10 % of Latin American-born beneficiaries return in old age [S125].
  2. Residency data must be read as new issues, not renewals. In 2024 the migration office issued 7,110 new residencies to all nationalities (3,677 temporary, 863 permanent, 371 definitive, 212 on the investor-pensioner-rentista track) against 56,780 renewals [S104]. US citizens hold 5.8 % of the temporary-plus-permanent file, so new US residencies are on the order of 250-400 a year [S171]. This segment cannot carry a project on its own.
  3. The channel that grows is property bought from abroad, and it buys finished homes. The Banreservas New York fair went from RD$3.1 bn of financing and about 480 properties in 2025 to RD$5.1 bn, 7,847 clients and 824 homes in 2026; 529 of those homes were finished and 64 % of the pre-qualified financing went to finished units [S007, S179]. Remittances reached US$11.9 bn in 2025 [S056].
  4. Capital families are the mountain market today. Jarabacoa's tourism activity concentrates in about 12 days a month and the town has more than 1,000 properties renting rooms [S126]; brokers describe limited buildable land and growing demand from wealthy families, whom the owner's own contacts place in Santiago and Santo Domingo [S046].
  5. Product norms. Dominican mountain projects sell lots with a catalogue of villa models, US$2,000-5,000 reservations, 20-30 % down and 6-12 monthly payments [S084, S034, S043, S036]. Costa Rican gated communities let buyers pick a builder under guidelines and charge US$100-500 a month in dues [S135]. Cap Cana approves every build in three phases [S091].
  6. Prices. Jarabacoa: median asking US$65/m² for 52 lots, US$85-110 in central gated projects; villas median US$304 k [S046, S047]. Constanza Hills: 97 lots of 2,000 m² or more at US$45-55/m² [S034]. Rancho Arriba: US$70/m² unserviced, raw land at US$3/m² [S036, project memory]. Building costs RD$40-46 k/m² standard to RD$60-70 k luxury (US$650-1,130) [S146].
  7. Rancho Arriba's position. The site is 95 km and about 1 h 20 min from Santo Domingo by the Duarte highway and Piedra Blanca (free-flow estimate) against 2 h to Jarabacoa or Constanza (derived); the province ran a homicide rate of 4.1 per 100,000 in January-May 2025, sixth lowest, against 7.7 nationally [S160]; the municipality has no cadastre or applied land-use plan [S117] and only eight approved lotification lots (project cadastre harvest). Weaknesses: no private clinic closer than Bonao, 46 min away [S163, S164]; a 1.3 million m² greenhouse economy around the field [S058]; no established community; unproven prices.
  8. Economics. A phased after-tax cash flow (section 8.4) shows: at today's local prices (US$60/m²) the 42-lot plan loses money in every case; at Jarabacoa parity (US$85/m² lots, US$120 estate lots) it returns US$0.55 M after tax and 12 % financing at six lots a year, US$1.35 M if the family funds it with equity, on a peak cash need of about US$2.8 M (US$1.9 M if model villas are built only against contracts); only the stretch case (US$110-150/m²) yields US$1.8-2.4 M. Buying the neighbour's parcel _1 adds US$0.8-2.1 M after tax at base-to-parity prices because its flat lots cost half as much to service, but it doubles the lots to sell.
  9. What has to be true for the base case: phase-1 lots sell at US$60-70/m² to 12-15 buyers within 18 months; a bank finances lot-plus-house packages for diaspora buyers; Parcela 2 has a title before anything on it is marketed; contractor quotes land within 20 % of the US$2.56 M infrastructure model; well yield covers 42 homes and a clubhouse.

Resumen ejecutivo (español)

  1. El flujo de jubilados es real pero estable. La Seguridad Social de EE. UU. paga 7,499 beneficiarios con dirección en RD (5,253 jubilados), US$8.4 millones al mes; el número lleva desde 2009 entre 7,176 y 7,696 [S158, S159].
  2. Las residencias hay que leerlas como nuevas, no renovaciones. En 2024 Migración emitió 7,110 residencias nuevas (212 por inversión, pensionados y rentistas) frente a 56,780 renovaciones [S104]; las nuevas a estadounidenses rondan 250-400 al año [S171]. Ese segmento no sostiene un proyecto solo.
  3. El canal que crece es la diáspora comprando casa terminada desde fuera: la feria Banreservas de Nueva York vendió 824 viviendas en marzo de 2026, 529 terminadas, y el 64 % del financiamiento fue a unidades terminadas [S179]. Remesas 2025: US$11,866 millones [S056].
  4. Las familias dominicanas adineradas son el mercado de montaña de hoy: dueñas de las villas de Jarabacoa (por conocimiento de corredores, sobre todo de la capital y Santiago), con actividad turística de unos 12 días al mes; el pueblo tiene más de 1,000 propiedades en alquiler [S046, S126].
  5. Formato dominante: solar más catálogo de villas, reserva US$2,000-5,000, 20-30 % inicial y 6-12 cuotas [S084, S034, S043, S036]. Recomendación: dos ofertas, paquete solar+casa con contrato a precio fijo para la diáspora, y solares con código de diseño y cuota de mantenimiento para las familias de la capital; villas modelo solo contra contrato; sin reloj de penalidad.
  6. Precios: Jarabacoa mediana US$65/m² (52 solares), US$85-110 en proyectos cerrados céntricos; villas mediana US$304 mil [S046, S047]. Constanza Hills: 97 lotes de 2,000 m² o más a US$45-55/m² [S034]. Rancho Arriba: US$70/m² sin servicios; tierra bruta US$3/m² [S036]. Construcción RD$40-70 mil/m² [S146].
  7. Rancho Arriba: 95 km y ~1 h 20 min desde Santo Domingo por la Duarte y Piedra Blanca (estimación en flujo libre) frente a 2 h a Jarabacoa o Constanza; provincia con 4.1 homicidios por 100,000 en enero-mayo 2025 (sexta más baja) [S160]; sin catastro ni plan de uso de suelo [S117]; sin clínica privada más cerca que Bonao (46 min); 1.3 millones de m² de invernaderos alrededor [S058].
  8. Economía: a los precios locales de hoy el plan de 42 solares pierde dinero; a paridad con Jarabacoa deja US$0.55 millones después de impuestos y financiamiento a seis solares por año (US$1.35 millones si la familia lo financia con capital propio), con una necesidad pico de caja de ~US$2.8 millones (US$1.9 millones si las villas modelo se construyen solo contra contrato); solo el caso "stretch" (US$110-150/m²) deja US$1.8-2.4 millones. Comprar la parcela _1 del vecino añade US$0.8-2.1 millones porque sus solares planos cuestan la mitad de urbanizar, pero duplica los solares por vender.
  9. Lo que tiene que ser cierto: vender 12-15 solares de fase 1 a US$60-70/m² en 18 meses; un banco que financie paquetes solar+casa a la diáspora; título de la Parcela 2 antes de vender nada en ella; cotizaciones de contratistas dentro del 20 % del modelo de US$2.56 millones; pozos que rindan para 42 casas y el club.

2.3 Stock of foreign-born, returnees and the diaspora

  • ENI-2017 counted 570,933 foreign-born residents: 10,016 born in the United States, 7,572 in Spain, 3,713 in Italy, 2,356 in Puerto Rico, 25,872 in Venezuela [S016]. UNDESA's 2015 stock had 25,814 US-born, a broader definition [S108].
  • The 2010 census identified 38,446 returnees; the INM survey of 509 returnees (55.1 % from the US) found 80.9 % had always planned to return. Reasons: deported 18.5 %, did not adapt 10.6 %, other family reasons 9.0 %, care of minors 8.6 %, care of sick or elderly relatives 7.3 %, already retired and wished to return 6.7 %, goal achieved 6.5 %, unemployment or crisis 5.9 %, health or old age 4.3 %, wanted to open a business 3.3 %. Among returnees over 50, "already retired" and "did not adapt" each account for 12 %. 53.6 % could not bring their families; 10.8 % own a business. Returnees cluster in Santo Domingo (24 %), Santiago (14.8 %) and the Distrito Nacional (14.5 %) [S003, S004].
  • The diaspora: 2,874,124 Dominicans abroad at end-2024 (2,398,009 in the US, 201,162 in Spain) [S077]; 3,030,647 in 129 countries in the fifth registry, 84.9 % in North America, 9.8 % in Europe, more than a third under 25 [S076]. In the US, 1.3 million Dominican-born, 55 % in greater New York, 75 % aged 18-64, median age 46, 21 % in poverty, 64 % speaking English less than very well [S009]. In Spain, 193,653 Dominican-born in 2023 and 239,400 including the nationalised, arriving at 9,136 a year in 2016-20 [S075]. In Puerto Rico the Dominican-born population has a median age of 49 [S141].
  • Intent: Gallup (2010) found 15 % of US Hispanics, 18 % of the foreign-born, would leave the US permanently [S122]. Social Security is portable for US citizens, the decisive enabler for the diaspora [S078].

2.4 The channel that is growing: homes bought from abroad

  • Banreservas fairs for the diaspora: 2024, RD$8.5 bn of requests for about 1,800 properties [S006]; May 2025, financing above RD$3,106 M for about 480 properties, 5,450 visitors, 10 % fixed for seven years, 90 % loan-to-value, 20 years [S007]; March 2026, RD$5,118 M (+65 %), 7,847 clients (+44 %), 914 pre-qualified (+56 %), 824 homes sold (+72 %), of which 529 finished (+90 %) and 295 under construction (+46 %); 64 % of the pre-qualified financing, RD$2,784 M, went to finished homes [S179, S180]. Exhibitors included a Jarabacoa apartment project [S179]. Nothing in the fair material shows loans for lots.
  • Remittances: US$11,866 M in 2025, +10.3 %, 80 % from the US, 64.2 % landing in metropolitan areas [S056, S057].
  • Developers report the diaspora's weight in sales is smaller than in the past, mid-high projects sell slowly, and unsold inventory grew in 2025-26 [S053, S051].

3. What buyers want, by segment

3.1 Santo Domingo and Santiago families (the mountain market today)

  • Ownership and use: tourism activity in Jarabacoa concentrates in about 12 days a month; more than 1,000 properties offer rooms; the cluster says it is receiving more Dominicans from abroad [S126]. The villa owners are wealthy Dominican families [S046]; that they come mainly from Santiago and Santo Domingo is the owner's and brokers' knowledge, not a published statistic. Brokers cite limited buildable land and rising demand from wealthy families, and warn that growth must be planned to avoid overpopulation and service failures [S046, S045].
  • Price band: villas listed at a median US$304,000 (48 of the province's 56 villa listings are in Jarabacoa); volume villas US$240-270 k (a six-villa project at US$240 k for 230 m² on 320 m², Quintas del Bosque US$250 k for 175 m² on 2,500 m², a furnished Pinar Dorado villa at US$270 k), turnkey packages US$160-350 k; luxury US$518 k to 3 M [S167, S166, S048, S043, S109].
  • Rental economics they check: La Vega province has 1,452 active short-term rentals at 32 % occupancy, US$137 average rate and US$14,500 a year per listing, but listings fell 43 % and rates 27 % year on year [S137].
  • Pain points they know from Jarabacoa: a river-fed aqueduct that stops when it rains, an unfinished road to Constanza, no applied territorial plan [S126, S050].

3.2 Diaspora households (buy now, use later)

  • They buy finished homes: 64 % of the 2026 fair's financing and 529 of 824 sales were finished units [S179]. The fair's product is a 90 % mortgage on a home, not a lot loan [S007, S179].
  • Family, not retirement, drives return; only 6.7 % of returnees came back already retired (12 % of those over 50) and 53.6 % could not bring their families [S003]. A 58-year-old in New Jersey planning a return in five years needs a trusted party to build, a clear title, dues they can budget, a clinic they trust, and proof that people are there on a Tuesday in February.
  • Ley 171-07 is a selling point for them, not only for foreigners: with a US$1,500 Social Security benefit they qualify for the transfer-tax exemption on the first property, 50 % IPI and the household-goods exemption [S001].
  • Financing exists for homes at 10 % fixed [S007]; whether a lot in a project qualifies must be confirmed with the bank (section 8.7).

3.3 Foreign retirees and remote workers (opportunistic)

  • What pioneers found: at Lake Chapala over 80 % cited housing affordability, over 50 % said the move was mainly economic and that affordable healthcare kept them; median pre-retirement income was US$46-50 k; 46.6 % owned and 37.3 % rented [S013]. Guanacaste's residential tourists are amenity-led and mobile [S081]. Panama wins the expat surveys on ease of settling, friendliness and cost [S121].
  • Age-in-place features: 75 % of US adults 50+ want to stay in their home, 62 % cite affordable housing and 56 % want housing that fits changing needs; 75 % still prefer a single-family home, smaller [S061, S062]; new homes have shrunk to 2,067 sq ft [S064].
  • In the DR this segment concentrates on the coast (Las Terrenas, Cabarete, Puerto Plata) and the DR ranks 20th in the 2026 retirement index [S095, S120]; for a mountain site without an English-speaking community it is a bonus, not a base case.

3.4 What has changed since the pioneer wave

  1. From cheap to safe and serviced: Costa Rica recorded 873 homicides in 2025 (16.7 per 100,000; 876 in 2024, 905 in 2023) and locals now perceive foreign residents as driving displacement [S189, S191, S067]; resales there take 360-420 days [S022]. Security, water, power, title and a clinic are the checklist.
  2. Peak 65: 4.1 million Americans turn 65 each year through 2027, 11,200 a day, and about half of those households are financially at risk [S143]; the Dominican diaspora rides the same wave with a median age of 46 [S009].
  3. Part-time living: Chapala's winter population is more than twice its permanent one [S013]; Jarabacoa works 12 days a month [S126]. The product must work as a lock-and-leave second home with rental management.

4.2 Reading

The Dominican norm is lot first, developer-offered villa models second, with small reservations and short interest-free plans. No Dominican mountain project in the archive charges builders a programme fee or runs a formal builder guild; the US examples are useful for design control, not as fee precedents. Merchant building captures the construction margin but carries cost risk in a market where direct costs rose 3.72 % in 2025 and about 30 % in 2020-21 [S144, S145]. No public benchmark for lot-development margins was found; the AdventuresinCRE template is a modelling tool, not a data source [S093].

4.3 Recommendation: two offers, one design code

  1. Diaspora package. Lot plus house under one fixed-price contract from a catalogue of three or four single-level models, staged payments matched to the fair's financing, construction management by the developer, and rental management on delivery. This is what the fair channel finances [S179] and what the Habitame and Loft Home formats already do [S043, S085].
  2. Capital-family lots. Design-coded lots (single or split level following the contour, zero-step entries, covered galleries, hot-tub court, roof and colour palette, setbacks, no perimeter walls above a set height, solar-ready roofs, rainwater and septic standards) with an architectural review committee, two or three vetted builders with published all-in prices per m² by finish level, and the right to bring another builder under the code with a quality bond. Holding cost is the HOA dues (Aval charges US$50 unbuilt, US$100 built [S036]), not a penalty clock; a 5-7 year build-by date protects the community without punishing the buyer who retires in five years.
  3. Model villas built to contract. Three or four developer-built villas per phase set the standard, but they are built against signed contracts, not on speculation; that cuts the peak cash need by about US$0.9 M (section 8.4).
  4. Fee income is upside, not base. A 5 % programme fee on builder contracts and a rental-management margin can add US$0.2-0.3 M over the project but have no Dominican precedent; the base pro forma treats them as upside.
  5. Legal sequencing. Lots sold before the subdivision is approved are contracts without a registrable title, and urban subdivisions with streets add 6-18 months of approvals [S130]; pre-sales must be reservations or promesas with escrow, and Parcela 2 needs a title before any lot on it is offered.

5. Five lessons from Costa Rica and the pioneers

  1. Communities take decades. Atenas, the Central Valley coffee town at about 700 m sold on climate, has about 2,500 full- or part-time expat residents [S114, S112]; Chapala's foreign community is more than a century old [S013]. A new Dominican site must supply the community itself (clubhouse operator, events, rental pool) rather than wait for it.
  2. Water and title gate every sale. Due diligence in Costa Rica runs 30-60 days and covers title, liens, zoning and water rights; building lots run US$50-200 k and gated homes US$400-750 k with dues of US$150-400 [S112]. The Dominican equivalent is the well-yield test, the urbanización parcelaria approval and the Parcela 2 deslinde [S130].
  3. Dues are a product feature. Costa Rican gated communities charge US$100-500 a month and buyers weigh the rules against security and convenience [S135]; Roca Verde's dues cover roads, wells and a guard [S134]; Cap Cana runs about US$2.65/m² a month in towers [S186]. Publish the dues and the budget behind them.
  4. The resale market is slow and price-sensitive. Costa Rican resales take 360-420 days and close 6-12 % under asking; US buyers paused in 2025 [S022, S021]. Foreign buyers were about 40 % of transactions and prices rose 7.8 % in 2024 [S099]. Plan absorption on the slow end.
  5. Safety and local acceptance decide the exit. Costa Rica's 873 homicides in 2025 (16.7 per 100,000) and the perception that foreign residents displace locals now push residents out [S189, S067, S118]. The DR's national rate was 7.7 in January-May 2025 and Ocoa's 4.1 [S160]; the project still needs 24 h security and property-crime data for Rancho Arriba (section 9).

Panama's Boquete shows the highland template: Valle Escondido, the gated golf community credited with starting the wave, lists homes around US$480,000 [S177, S023]. Law 9996 in Costa Rica cut the investor threshold to US$150,000 and added duty-free household goods, vehicles and a 20 % transfer-tax exemption, locked for ten years for early applicants [S017, S073, S071].

7. Site context and positioning

  • Access. By road the site is 95 km from Santo Domingo via the Duarte highway to Piedra Blanca and 32 km of mountain road; the router's free-flow time is 1 h 20 min, against 2 h 02 to Jarabacoa and 2 h 01 to Constanza; Santiago 118 km, Las Américas airport 126 km, Cibao airport 109 km, Bonao 48 km, Ocoa 30 km (derived, research/data/drive_times_osrm.csv; real times need a Google Maps check at peak hours). The southern approach through Baní and the 11 km Rancho Arriba-Nizao road opened in December 2023 [S058]; the Ocoa-Rancho Arriba-Piedra Blanca corridor was rebuilt for about US$50 M [S059]. The April 2026 landslides were on the Ocoa-Cruce de Ocoa stretch south of Ocoa town, not on either approach used by Santo Domingo buyers [S060].
  • Elevation and climate. Rancho Arriba town sits at 475 m with a mean of 21-25 °C [S097]; the ADOMPRETUR guide's "700 m" describes the upland where the site lies [S127]; the site itself is at about 704 m with a hill to 842 m (project survey). That is between Jarabacoa (about 530 m) and Constanza (1,200-1,300 m).
  • Neighbours. The field sits in the country's greenhouse belt, about 1.3 million m² of greenhouses in Rancho Arriba [S058]: spraying, plastic, pumps and trucks on the access road are part of the setting and need buffers, orientation and a farm-to-table story rather than silence.
  • Services. Rancho Arriba's municipal hospital was remodeled and reopened in December 2023 [S058]; the provincial hospital is in Ocoa, 37 min [S138]; two private clinics in Bonao (46 min) offer 24 h emergency, intensive care, dialysis and imaging between them [S163, S164]; tertiary care is in Santo Domingo, 1 h 20 min or more. Power is EDESUR with scheduled maintenance outages [S162]. The municipality has no cadastre, only a draft urban plan, and more than half of district households use latrines [S117].
  • Safety. In January-May 2025 the province ran 4.10 homicides per 100,000, the sixth lowest (Puerto Plata 3.3, Sánchez Ramírez 3.4, Dajabón 3.7, two provinces at zero) against 7.7 nationally; the last full year in the archive is 11.15 (2023) [S160, S161]. No property-crime data for Rancho Arriba was found; a lock-and-leave product needs it.
  • Regulatory. Ocoa is an "ecotourism province" under Law 151-04 [S127]. CONFOTUR classifies tourism and "turístico-inmobiliario" projects counted in hotel-type rooms, with a corporate object limited to the tourism project and environmental licensing; lot sales to end users are not a classified activity and the benefit stops at the first buyer [S183, S184, S185, S133]. Only an ecolodge or boutique-hotel component could qualify; the base pro forma assumes no CONFOTUR benefit.

Segments, in order of likely absorption. (1) Santo Domingo and Santiago families, 45-65, second or pre-retirement home, want a 1 h 20 min drive, security, a pool and a place their parents can use; comp: Jarabacoa villa band US$160-650 k [S046, S043, S048, S109]. (2) Diaspora households in New York, New Jersey, Boston, Madrid and San Juan, 50-65, planning a return in 5-10 years, buying a lot-plus-house package financed at the fair [S179]. (3) Foreign retirees and remote workers, opportunistic, through the diaspora's networks and Ley 171-07.

Price ladder to test. Phase-1 villa lots US$60-70/m² (1,000-1,600 m², US$65-110 k), the Rancho Arriba comp [S036]; later phases US$85-95/m² if phase 1 sells; estate lots of 2,000-3,000 m² at US$60-90/m² (Constanza Hills sells 2,000 m² lots at US$45-55 [S034]), US$120 only as a stretch; model villas 150-220 m² at US$1,000-1,130 per built m² plus lot and 10 % soft costs, so a 200 m² villa on a 1,300 m² lot at US$85 lands near US$330-360 k, the same money that buys a finished villa in Jarabacoa today [S146, S046].

8. The project concept

8.1 Programme (Scenario A, family land, 17.9 ha)

  • 36 villa lots of about 1,300 m² on the field in pocket clusters around shared greens on a car-light inner ring; six estate lots of about 2,500 m² on the upper edge of parcel _3 at the toe of the hill. Open item: none of the concept layouts A-H (18-43 lots within the 8.7 ha envelope under 15 % slope) has yet placed six 2,500 m² view lots on parcel _3; one layout run must confirm the 42-lot count before its revenue is quoted as feasible. The estate lots move onto Parcela 2 only after its deslinde and title, and only if the design brief's decision to keep Parcela 2 as commons is revisited; every lot gets a zero-step pad (design brief, project memory).
  • Core: clubhouse with pool, gym and mirador terrace at about 720 m, lagoon below it, communal garden on a greenhouse, fenced playground and dog park inside the gate, commercial plaza outside the gate at the neck; trails and a mirador on Parcela 2; perimeter trail behind the lots.
  • Infrastructure: two wells, tank and pumps, water treatment, on-site sewer plant, underground power with a solar-plus-storage microgrid for the core (costed separately at US$0.25 M), fibre, concrete streets on grades above 10 %, gate and 24 h security.
  • Governance: condominium or HOA regime recorded with the urbanización parcelaria, design code and review committee, rental-management service, published dues budget (8.5).

8.2 Legal and permitting path

Deslinde of Parcela 2, then refundición with parcel _3 and one urbanización parcelaria file (agrimensor, Regional Mensuras, Tribunal de Tierras, Registro de Títulos); simple subdivisions take 3-8 months, urban ones with streets add 6-18 months of municipal and utility approvals [S129, S130]. MIVHED lotification certificate with sealed plans and a descriptive memory under the Building Code, ayuntamiento land-use approval, Medio Ambiente notification or licence, MOPC, INAPA and EDESUR approvals when streets and services are opened [S128, S130, S092]. Pre-sales before approval must be reservations or promesas with escrow [S130].

8.3 Phasing

  • Phase 1 (years 0-2): gate, main street and drainage, wells, tank and treatment core, power and fibre trunk, clubhouse shell, 14 lots, model villas built only against contracts. Pre-sell 40 % as escrowed reservations through the Banreservas fair (March) and two Santiago and Santo Domingo brokerages before breaking ground.
  • Phase 2 (years 2-5): remaining field lots once the tenth lot sells, lagoon, plaza. Phase 3 (years 4-7): estate lots and hill trails once the 24th lot sells. Scenario B after A sells through.
  • Absorption: 6 lots a year is the planning case; Jarabacoa lists 52 lots and 48 villas at once, Constanza Hills put 97 lots on the market in August 2024, and pre-sales slowed in 2025-26 [S046, S034, S028, S051]. Four lots a year leaves the project unsold after nine years in every price case.
